The One-Pot Stew Guide

A good stew is mostly patience. These five steps work for almost any combination of meat, beans and vegetables.

Kitchen notes

  • Heavy pot with a lid
  • Tough, flavourful cuts are best
  • Allow 2–3 hours for beef or lamb
  • Season at the end, not just the start

IChoose the right cut

Stews love tougher, well-used cuts: beef chuck or shin, lamb shoulder, pork shoulder, chicken thighs. Their connective tissue melts into gelatine during a long simmer and makes the sauce silky. Lean steaks turn dry and stringy.

IIBrown in batches

Pat the meat dry and brown it in a hot pot with a little oil, a few pieces at a time. Crowding makes it steam instead of colour. Those browned bits on the bottom are flavour; you will lift them later.

IIIBuild the base

Lower the heat, soften onions, carrots and celery in the fat, then add garlic, spices and tomato paste for a minute. Pour in a splash of stock and scrape the bottom of the pot clean.

IVSimmer low and slow

Return the meat, add liquid just to cover, bring to a simmer and lower the heat until only a bubble or two breaks the surface. Cover, leaving a small gap. Beef and lamb take 2–3 hours; chicken thighs about 45 minutes; beans and lentils 30–60.

VFinish and rest

Add quick-cooking vegetables in the last 20 minutes. Taste, then season with salt, a squeeze of lemon or a spoon of vinegar to brighten it. Like most stews, it tastes even better the next day.
